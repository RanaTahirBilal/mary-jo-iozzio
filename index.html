About

A moral theologian of inclusion

Mary Jo Iozzio has taught moral theology in the Clough School of Theology and Ministry at Boston College since 2013. Before that she spent twenty years as a professor at Barry University in Miami Shores, Florida, and taught as an adjunct at Fordham University, Providence College and the University of Rhode Island.

She is widely regarded as one of the first scholars to bring disability studies into Catholic moral theology. Her research and teaching center on intersectionality, Catholic social teaching and the flourishing of people with disabilities, and on the full personhood of every person.

Beyond the classroom

Service and advocacy

She has served on the board of directors of NETWORK Lobby for Catholic Social Justice, on the Leadership Council on Interfaith Group Policy Initiatives and on the ethics advisory group of Bon Secours Healthcare System. In 2021 she was part of Boston College's first delegation to COP26, the United Nations climate conference.

Closer to home she is a lector and eucharistic minister in her parish, volunteers on Saturday mornings at the Boston Food Bank, and joins local park cleanups.

Current work

She is writing essays on disability justice, including accessibility in the national parks, and plans future volumes in fundamental moral theology on the theological virtues, read through the work of the Belgian scholar Odon Lottin.
